## Search Strategies That Actually Work

Here's where most guides get vague. "Look carefully" is not advice. What follows are concrete scanning patterns you can apply to any dense search field, including the ones in this game.

### The grid sweep

Divide the screen into a mental grid — thirds horizontally, thirds vertically, nine zones total. Clear one zone completely before moving to the next. This is the single most reliable technique in any search game because it eliminates double-checking, which is where most of your wasted time goes.

### The edge-first pass

Targets are statistically more likely to hide in visually busy areas, and edges, corners, and overlapping textures are the busiest parts of any scene. Do a full perimeter pass before you touch the center.

### The color-break method

Hay is uniform. A needle isn't — it has a metallic sheen, a hard specular highlight, or a slight color temperature difference. Stop looking for a *shape* and start looking for a *break in the pattern*. This one shift in attention is often the difference between minutes and seconds.

| Pattern | How to Execute | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|
| Grid sweep | Split screen into nine zones, clear each fully | Always — your default method |
| Edge-first pass | Scan all borders and corners before the center | Large, dense fields |
| Color-break scan | Hunt for sheen and contrast, not silhouette | Target blends into texture |
| Reverse scan | Start bottom-right and work backward | You've already swept normally |
| Zoom-and-drag | Move in close and pan slowly across | Late-stage, heavily camouflaged areas |

### Take real breaks

Player experience with dense search games consistently shows that fatigue degrades visual scanning long before it degrades your patience. If you've swept the same area three times with no result, stand up for two minutes. Tunnel vision is real, and it usually resolves the moment you look away and come back.
