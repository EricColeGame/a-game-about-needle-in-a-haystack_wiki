## A Game About Needle In A Haystack How to Play: The Core Loop

Every session follows the same four-step rhythm, whether you are five minutes in or fifty. Understanding the loop matters more than memorizing any single trick, because the loop is what keeps you from spiraling into frustrated random clicking.

1. **Survey before you search.** Spend the first moments looking at the haystack as a whole. Note the boundaries, the densest regions, and any area that looks structurally different.
2. **Divide the field.** Split the haystack into imaginary zones — quadrants, horizontal bands, or rings. Never search without knowing which zone you are in.
3. **Sweep one zone at a time.** Cover it completely before moving on. Partial sweeps of many zones is the single most common reason players miss the needle.
4. **Log your progress mentally.** When you finish a zone, say it. "Top-left done." This prevents the disorienting loop of re-checking the same area.

That is the whole game. The difficulty is entirely in executing it without drifting.

## Search Patterns Compared

There is no single correct way to sweep a haystack, but some patterns suit certain situations better than others. Community reports suggest most experienced players settle into one primary pattern and switch only when it fails.

| Pattern | How it works | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Row-by-row sweep | Move across in horizontal bands, top to bottom | Small to medium haystacks | Fatigue near the bottom rows |
| Quadrant lock | Divide into four zones, fully clear each one | Large, open haystacks | Losing track of zone boundaries |
| Edge-first | Search the perimeter, then work inward | Haystacks with visible borders | Neglecting the dense center |
| Spiral | Start at the center and circle outward | Roughly circular piles | Overlapping rings cause double-checks |
| Column scan | Vertical strips, left to right | Tall, narrow views | Slow on wide screens |

### When to switch patterns

Switch the moment you catch yourself re-checking an area you already cleared. That is not persistence — it is your attention resetting and losing the thread. Changing patterns forces your eyes to process the field differently, which is often enough to make an overlooked detail pop.
