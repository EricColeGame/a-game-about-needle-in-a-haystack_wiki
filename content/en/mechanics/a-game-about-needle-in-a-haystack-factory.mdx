## The Core Mechanics Behind Every Search

Strip away the theme and the game runs on four interlocking systems: **scanning**, **extraction**, **valuation**, and **reinvestment**. Scanning is how you convert a haystack into information. Extraction is how information becomes an object in your inventory. Valuation turns objects into currency. Reinvestment turns currency into fewer future searches. Break any one of those links and progress stalls — which is exactly why players who only optimize scanning tend to plateau hard around the mid-game.

| Mechanic | What it actually does | Why it matters | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scanning | Reveals or narrows candidate zones inside a haystack | Converts time into usable information | Sweeping randomly instead of systematically |
| Extraction | Pulls a confirmed needle out of the pile | Turns a find into a resource | Abandoning a hot zone too early |
| Valuation | Assigns worth to what you found | Determines your income per search | Ignoring low-tier finds that fund upgrades |
| Reinvestment | Spends income on speed, reach, or capacity | Reduces future search time | Hoarding currency for a "perfect" purchase |

The important insight is that these systems compound. A 10% improvement to scanning feels tiny until you apply it to a hundred haystacks, at which point it becomes the difference between a productive evening and a wasted one. ### The throughput formula worth memorizing

You do not need exact numbers to reason about this. Just hold one idea in your head:

**Needles per hour = (haystacks processed per hour) × (needles found per haystack)**

Both terms matter, and most players only ever push the second one. If you double your haystack count but halve your hit rate, you have gained nothing but fatigue. The best upgrades in a search-driven progression are the ones that improve the *first* term without touching the second — because they scale with everything you already do well.

## Upgrade Priorities That Actually Pay Off

Upgrades in a search game fall into a predictable hierarchy, and buying out of order is the single most common reason runs feel sluggish. Use the table below as a spending order, not a shopping list.

| Priority | Upgrade category | Payoff profile | Buy when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Search speed / scan radius | Immediate, linear | Always — this is never a bad purchase |
| 2 | Inventory or carry capacity | Prevents wasted trips | Your runs end because you're full, not because you're done |
| 3 | Sell value or yield per needle | Multiplies existing effort | You can reliably fill capacity every run |
| 4 | Automation / batch processing | Delayed, exponential | Your manual loop is already near-optimal |
| 5 | Cosmetic or prestige-style upgrades | Vanity or long-term reset value | Last, and only with surplus |

A useful rule of thumb: if an upgrade does not change how you spend the next ten minutes, it is not a priority. Speed changes your next ten minutes. A slightly higher sale price on a needle you would have found anyway usually does not.

Also resist the urge to buy everything the moment it appears. In most progression systems, the third tier of an upgrade costs disproportionately more than the second, and the marginal gain is smaller. Buying wide — many cheap, early tiers — beats buying deep almost every time in the early game.

## Five Mistakes That Stall Your Factory

These show up repeatedly in player experience threads and are all fixable within a single session.

- **Searching without a system.** Random sweeping feels productive and is not. Pick a pattern — rows, quadrants, spiral — and finish it before switching.
- **Abandoning a hot zone.** If two finds came out of the same region, the odds are better there than anywhere else. Clear it.
- **Ignoring low-tier finds.** Small finds fund the upgrades that produce big finds. Skipping them is borrowing against your own future.
- **Over-scaling.** More haystacks per session is only good if your per-haystack time is already low.
- **Never selling.** Inventory full is a soft progress cap. If you are constantly bumping it, your bottleneck is bookkeeping, not searching.

The A Game About Needle In A Haystack factory mindset fixes all five at once, because every one of them is a throughput problem rather than a luck problem.
