## Why Console Ports Take Time for Small Games

Understanding the pipeline helps you judge rumors more accurately. A console release isn't a single button press; it's a separate certification process, a separate controller scheme, a separate set of platform requirements, and often a separate budget.

For a title like A Game About Needle In A Haystack, console work usually follows a predictable order of operations:

- **PC launch and stabilization.** Bug fixes, performance patches, and community feedback come first.
- **Sales data review.** The developer looks at whether the audience is large enough to justify porting costs.
- **Porting partner or self-port.** Small teams often hand console work to a specialist studio.
- **Certification.** Each platform holder runs its own compliance review, which can take weeks and sometimes requires changes.
- **Store page and announcement.** Only at this stage does a public listing typically appear.

That sequence explains why a game can be "coming to consoles" for a year without a single store page. It also explains why a store page appearing is such a strong signal — it's near the end of the chain, not the beginning.

|---|---|---|
| Official Steam page mentioning consoles | High | The Steam listing itself |
| Developer post on their own channel | High | The developer's official site or social account |
| Platform holder's own store listing | Very high | PlayStation Store, Microsoft Store, Nintendo eShop |
| "Leaked" database entry | Low to medium | Cross-check against the above |
